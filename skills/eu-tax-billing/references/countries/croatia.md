# Croatia — billing / quotes / VAT

## Overview
For freelancers, consultants, contractors, and small companies billing from Croatia.

## VAT basics
- Standard VAT: **25%**.
- Common reduced rates: **13%** and **5%** in specific sectors; most professional services stay at 25%.
- Croatia has a small-business / VAT-exemption framework, but thresholds and wording should be verified before relying on no-VAT treatment.

## Cross-border rule of thumb
- **B2B services to another EU business**: usually invoice **without Croatian VAT** under reverse charge if the customer's country is the place of supply.
- Include VAT IDs where relevant.
- Typical wording: `Reverse charge — Article 196 of Council Directive 2006/112/EC`.

## B2C / digital note
- **B2C digital services** generally follow customer-location VAT and can make **OSS** relevant quickly.

## Invoice fields / wording notes
Usually core / likely mandatory in practice:
- invoice number and issue date
- seller legal name/address
- seller VAT ID if VAT-registered
- customer legal name/address
- customer VAT ID for intra-EU B2B reverse-charge cases
- description, taxable amount, VAT treatment, total

Recommended practical fields:
- service period
- due date and payment terms
- contact details / bank details

If using a Croatian no-VAT / small-business wording, verify the exact local text first. Keep the split clear between core invoice facts and recommended extras, because the final field list can differ by regime.

## Quote notes
- State validity period and whether figures are **net** or **VAT included**.
- Add scope, exclusions, and deposit terms if relevant.

## Sole trader / company note
- The billing issue that matters most is VAT status, not just entity form.

## Filing cadence / admin rhythm
- VAT filing is often **monthly** once registered, though special situations can differ.
- Keep evidence for intra-EU B2B treatment and any OSS reporting.

## Escalate when
- the seller is near the exemption threshold
- mixed domestic and cross-border revenue appears
- exact Croatian exemption wording is needed

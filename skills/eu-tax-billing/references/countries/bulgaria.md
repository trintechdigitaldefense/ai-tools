# Bulgaria — billing / quotes / VAT

## Overview
For freelancers, consultants, contractors, and small companies billing from Bulgaria.

## VAT basics
- Standard VAT: **20%**.
- Key reduced rate: **9%** in limited sectors; most professional services stay at 20%.
- Bulgaria has a domestic VAT-registration threshold and separate cross-border triggers; verify the current threshold and any non-established / digital-service triggers before relying on non-registration.

## Cross-border rule of thumb
- **B2B services to another EU business**: often invoice **without Bulgarian VAT** under reverse charge when the customer's country is the place of supply.
- Include VAT IDs where relevant.
- Typical wording: `Reverse charge — Article 196 of Council Directive 2006/112/EC`.

## B2C / digital note
- **B2C digital services** usually follow customer-location VAT. OSS may matter quickly if selling digital services cross-border.

## Invoice fields / wording notes
Usually core / likely mandatory in practice:
- sequential invoice number and issue date
- seller legal name/address
- seller VAT ID if registered
- customer legal name/address
- customer VAT ID for reverse-charge B2B cases
- description, tax base, VAT treatment, total

Recommended practical fields:
- service period
- payment terms
- contact details and bank details

Treat the list above as practical guidance. If a Bulgarian registration number, local-language wording, or special exemption text is needed, verify before finalizing.

## Quote notes
- Make clear whether pricing is **without VAT** or **VAT included**.
- Include validity period, scope, and any deposit or milestone terms.

## Sole trader / company note
- Do not assume a business can omit VAT purely because it is small; confirm registration status and any cross-border registration trigger.

## Filing cadence / admin rhythm
- VAT returns are commonly **monthly** once registered.
- Keep records supporting intra-EU supplies and any OSS position.

## Escalate when
- the seller is near the Bulgarian registration threshold
- cross-border services are being supplied before domestic registration is clear
- local wording or reporting expectations are uncertain

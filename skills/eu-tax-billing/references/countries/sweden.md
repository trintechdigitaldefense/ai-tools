# Sweden — billing / quotes / VAT

## Overview
For freelancers, consultants, contractors, agencies, and small companies billing from Sweden.

## VAT basics
- Standard VAT: **25%**.
- Reduced rates exist in specific sectors; most professional services use 25%.
- Sweden has a domestic VAT-registration threshold and special cases; verify the current threshold before relying on non-registration.

## Cross-border rule of thumb
- **B2B services to another EU business**: usually invoice **without Swedish VAT** under reverse charge if the customer's country is the place of supply.
- Include VAT IDs where relevant.
- Typical wording: `Reverse charge — Article 196 of Council Directive 2006/112/EC`.

## B2C / digital note
- **B2C digital services** generally follow customer-location VAT and may require **OSS**.

## Invoice fields / wording notes
Usually core / likely mandatory in practice:
- invoice number and issue date
- seller legal name/address
- seller VAT number if registered
- customer legal name/address
- customer VAT number for reverse-charge B2B cases
- description, taxable amount, VAT treatment, total

Recommended practical fields:
- service period
- due date / payment terms
- contact details and bank details

Use this as a practical checklist. If the seller is below threshold or using a Swedish exemption, verify the exact wording before finalizing.

## Quote notes
- Clarify whether prices are ex VAT or inclusive of VAT.
- Add validity period, scope, exclusions, and any deposit terms.

## Sole trader / company note
- Confirm VAT-registration status separately from legal form.

## Filing cadence / admin rhythm
- VAT filing can be **monthly, quarterly, or annually** depending on turnover and status.
- Keep support for intra-EU B2B treatment and any OSS sales.

## Escalate when
- the seller is near the threshold
- exact Swedish wording is required
- filing cadence or digital-reporting workflow matters operationally

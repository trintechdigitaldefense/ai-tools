# Estonia — billing / quotes / VAT

## Overview
For freelancers, consultants, contractors, and small companies billing from Estonia.

## VAT basics
- Standard VAT: **22%**.
- Reduced rates exist in limited sectors; most professional services use the standard rate.
- Estonia has a domestic VAT-registration threshold and separate cross-border triggers; verify current thresholds before relying on non-registration.

## Cross-border rule of thumb
- **B2B services to another EU business**: usually invoice **without Estonian VAT** under reverse charge if the customer's country is the place of supply.
- Include VAT IDs where relevant.
- Typical wording: `Reverse charge — Article 196 of Council Directive 2006/112/EC`.

## B2C / digital note
- **B2C digital services** generally follow customer-location VAT and can make **OSS** relevant quickly.

## Invoice fields / wording notes
Usually core / likely mandatory in practice:
- invoice number and issue date
- seller legal name/address
- seller VAT ID if registered
- customer legal name/address
- customer VAT ID for intra-EU B2B reverse-charge cases
- description, taxable amount, VAT treatment, total

Recommended practical fields:
- service period
- payment terms
- email / phone / bank details

If the seller is not VAT-registered, verify whether the invoice should simply omit VAT or use specific wording. Do not guess local mandatory extras without confirmation.

## Quote notes
- Clarify whether prices exclude VAT or include VAT.
- Add validity period, scope, and any upfront-payment terms.

## Sole trader / company note
- Confirm VAT-registration status separately from legal form.

## Filing cadence / admin rhythm
- VAT filing is commonly **monthly** once registered.
- Keep records supporting intra-EU treatment and any OSS sales.

## Escalate when
- domestic threshold or cross-border trigger is close
- the seller uses Estonian e-residency structures but registration facts are unclear
- exact wording or filing obligations need certainty

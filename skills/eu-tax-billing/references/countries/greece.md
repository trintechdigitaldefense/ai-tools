# Greece — billing / quotes / VAT

## Overview
For freelancers, consultants, contractors, and small companies billing from Greece.

## VAT basics
- Standard VAT: **24%**.
- Reduced rates exist in specific sectors and some geographic contexts; most professional services use 24%.
- Greece has domestic VAT-registration rules and special cases; verify any threshold or exemption relied on before drafting no-VAT wording.

## Cross-border rule of thumb
- **B2B services to another EU business**: usually invoice **without Greek VAT** under reverse charge if the customer's country is the place of supply.
- Include VAT IDs where relevant.
- Typical wording: `Reverse charge — Article 196 of Council Directive 2006/112/EC`.

## B2C / digital note
- **B2C digital services** generally follow customer-location VAT and may require **OSS**.

## Invoice fields / wording notes
Usually core / likely mandatory in practice:
- invoice number and issue date
- seller legal name/address
- seller VAT ID if registered
- customer legal name/address
- customer VAT ID for reverse-charge B2B cases
- description, tax base, VAT treatment, total

Recommended practical fields:
- service period
- payment terms
- contact details / bank details

Keep the split clear between core invoice facts and recommended operational extras. Platform or bookkeeping setup can still affect the final field list, so verify exact Greek exemption wording before finalizing.

## Quote notes
- State whether prices are net or VAT included.
- Add validity period, scope, assumptions, and deposit terms.

## Sole trader / company note
- Confirm actual VAT-registration status and invoicing workflow; entity form alone is not enough.

## Filing cadence / admin rhythm
- VAT filing is often **monthly or quarterly** depending on books and status.
- Keep evidence for intra-EU B2B treatment and any OSS sales.

## Escalate when
- local e-invoicing / platform rules affect invoice generation
- threshold or exemption status is uncertain
- exact Greek wording is required

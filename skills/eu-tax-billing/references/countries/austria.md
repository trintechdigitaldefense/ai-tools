# Austria — billing / quotes / VAT

## Overview
For freelancers, consultants, contractors, agencies, and small companies billing from Austria.

## VAT basics
- Standard VAT: **20%**.
- Common reduced rates: **13%** and **10%** in specific sectors; most professional services stay at 20%.
- Austria has a small-business **Kleinunternehmer** regime; thresholds and recent reforms should be verified before relying on no-VAT wording.

## Cross-border rule of thumb
- **B2B services to another EU business**: usually invoice **without Austrian VAT** under reverse charge if the customer's country is the place of supply.
- Include VAT IDs where relevant.
- Typical wording: `Reverse charge — Article 196 of Council Directive 2006/112/EC`.

## B2C / digital note
- **B2C digital services** generally follow customer-location VAT and may require **OSS** once cross-border B2C turnover is relevant.

## Invoice fields / wording notes
Usually core / likely mandatory in practice:
- invoice number and issue date
- seller legal name and address
- seller VAT ID if VAT-registered
- customer legal name and address
- customer VAT ID for intra-EU B2B reverse-charge cases
- description of services, taxable amount, VAT treatment, total

Recommended practical fields:
- service period
- payment terms
- email / phone / bank details

If relying on Austria's small-business no-VAT regime, use the correct local wording and verify the current threshold. Local practice can differ; confirm if a registration number or extra wording is required.

## Quote notes
- State whether prices are **net** or **VAT included**.
- Add validity period, scope, exclusions, and any deposit requirement.

## Sole trader / company note
- The main billing difference is whether the seller is under the small-business regime or regular VAT. Do not infer that from entity type alone.

## Filing cadence / admin rhythm
- VAT filing is commonly **monthly or quarterly**, with annual reconciliation in many cases.
- Keep support for intra-EU B2B treatment and any OSS sales.

## Escalate when
- the seller is near the small-business threshold
- the invoice needs Austrian exemption wording you are not sure about
- e-invoicing or platform reporting rules affect the workflow

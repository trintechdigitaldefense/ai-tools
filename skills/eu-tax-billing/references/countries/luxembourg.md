# Luxembourg — billing / quotes / VAT

## Overview
For freelancers, consultants, small agencies, solo founders, and service companies billing from Luxembourg.

## VAT basics
- Standard VAT: **17%**.
- Key reduced rates sometimes seen on invoices: **14%**, **8%**, **3%**; most professional services use 17%.
- Domestic VAT registration threshold: commonly cited at **€35,000** annual turnover for Luxembourg-established businesses; verify if relying on a new-law increase.

## Cross-border rule of thumb
- **B2B services to another EU business**: usually invoice **without Luxembourg VAT** under reverse charge when the customer's country is the place of supply.
- Include both VAT numbers where relevant.
- Typical wording: `Reverse charge — Article 196 of Council Directive 2006/112/EC`.

## B2C / digital note
- **B2C digital services** usually follow the customer's country VAT rate. OSS may be the cleanest route once cross-border B2C digital turnover becomes relevant.

## Invoice fields / wording notes
Usually core / likely mandatory in practice:
- invoice number and date
- seller name/address and VAT number if registered
- customer name/address and VAT number for B2B reverse-charge cases
- clear description of services and service period
- taxable base, VAT rate or exemption note, total payable
- payment terms

Recommended practical fields:
- service period if useful
- email / phone / bank details

Treat the list above as a practical split between likely mandatory/core details and commonly useful extras. If a local exemption wording, registration number, or country-specific field is uncertain, verify before finalizing.

## Quote notes
- Make clear whether the quote is **VAT included or excluded**.
- State validity period and whether a deposit is required.

## Sole trader / company note
- Luxembourg companies often use Lux-company details exactly as registered; make sure the legal form is correct on invoices and quotes.

## Filing cadence / admin rhythm
- VAT returns can be **annual, quarterly, or monthly** depending on turnover.
- Keep support for intra-EU B2B treatment and any OSS records.

## Escalate when
- turnover is near the exemption threshold
- the business has mixed Luxembourg and foreign place-of-supply revenue
- the user is relying on a possible threshold reform or special reduced-rate treatment

# Ireland — billing / quotes / VAT

## Overview
For Irish freelancers, consultants, contractors, agencies, and small companies billing from Ireland.

## VAT basics
- Standard VAT: **23%**.
- Key reduced service-relevant rates sometimes seen: **13.5%** and **9%**; most professional services use 23%.
- Domestic VAT registration threshold: commonly **€42,500 for services** and **€85,000 for goods-heavy trading**.

## Cross-border rule of thumb
- **B2B services to another EU business**: usually invoice **without Irish VAT** under reverse charge when the customer is the place of supply.
- Include VAT numbers where relevant.
- Typical wording: `Reverse charge — Article 196 of Council Directive 2006/112/EC`.

## B2C / digital note
- **B2C digital services** generally follow customer-country VAT. OSS may be the practical route once the EU cross-border threshold is exceeded.

## Invoice fields / wording notes
Usually core / likely mandatory in practice:
- supplier name/address and VAT number if registered
- customer name/address and VAT number for reverse-charge B2B cases
- invoice number and date
- clear description and tax point/service period where useful
- net amount, VAT rate/amount or no-VAT wording, total due
- payment terms

Recommended practical fields:
- service period if useful
- email / phone / bank details

Treat the list above as a practical split between likely mandatory/core details and commonly useful extras. If a local exemption wording, registration number, or country-specific field is uncertain, verify before finalizing.

## Quote notes
- State whether the quote is **VAT exclusive** or **VAT inclusive**.
- Add validity period, scope assumptions, and deposit / staged-payment terms if used.

## Sole trader / company note
- Irish contractors often switch between sole trader and limited company structures, but for invoicing the immediate issue is usually whether Irish VAT registration has been triggered and whether the customer is B2B with a valid VAT number.

## Filing cadence / admin rhythm
- Irish VAT filing is often on a **bi-monthly** pattern, though other frequencies can apply.
- Keep evidence for intra-EU B2B treatment and OSS records where relevant.

## Escalate when
- the seller is near the services threshold
- supplies mix Irish domestic work with foreign place-of-supply work
- digital services, grants, or unusual exempt activities muddy the VAT position

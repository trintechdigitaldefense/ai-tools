# Czechia — billing / quotes / VAT

## Overview
For freelancers, consultants, contractors, agencies, and small companies billing from Czechia.

## VAT basics
- Standard VAT: **21%**.
- Reduced rates: **12%** and historically **15%** under older structures; verify if a reduced rate matters to the transaction. Most professional services stay at 21%.
- Czech VAT-registration thresholds and special cross-border registration triggers have changed in recent years; verify current limits before relying on non-registration.

## Cross-border rule of thumb
- **B2B services to another EU business**: usually invoice **without Czech VAT** under reverse charge if the customer's country is the place of supply.
- Include VAT IDs where relevant.
- Typical wording: `Reverse charge — Article 196 of Council Directive 2006/112/EC`.

## B2C / digital note
- **B2C digital services** generally follow customer-location VAT and may require **OSS**.

## Invoice fields / wording notes
Usually core / likely mandatory in practice:
- invoice number and issue date
- seller legal name/address
- seller VAT ID if VAT-registered
- customer legal name/address
- customer VAT ID for intra-EU B2B reverse-charge cases
- description, taxable amount, VAT treatment, total

Recommended practical fields:
- service period
- due date / payment terms
- contact details and bank details

Mandatory versus recommended fields can depend on whether the seller is VAT-registered and on local bookkeeping practice. Verify exact Czech wording if using a no-VAT regime.

## Quote notes
- State whether prices are net or VAT included.
- Add validity period, assumptions, and any prepayment milestones.

## Sole trader / company note
- VAT-registration status is the key billing issue; do not infer it from legal form.

## Filing cadence / admin rhythm
- VAT filing can be **monthly or quarterly** depending on status.
- Preserve evidence for intra-EU B2B supplies and any OSS sales.

## Escalate when
- the seller is near the registration threshold
- a reduced rate may apply
- exact Czech exemption wording or reporting duty is uncertain
